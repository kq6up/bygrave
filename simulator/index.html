Start with the sight

How LAT, DEC, and LHA meet the scales

LAT is your position north or south of the equator. DEC is the observed body's position north or south of the sky's equator. LHA tells how far the body is west or east of your local meridian.

  1. Put DEC under L, the long pointer, on the inner cotangent scale.
  2. Move the cursor so LHA is under S, the short pointer, on the middle cosine scale.
  3. Read the intermediate angle y at L on the cotangent scale. Then use latitude: convert it to co-latitude (90° − |LAT|) and combine that with y. Same-name LAT/DEC uses addition; opposite-name uses subtraction, with special cases handled by the historical instructions.

What the scale names mean: cotangent and cosine are mathematical functions that turn those angles into positions along the graduated strips. They are the instrument's way of doing the multiplication/division in the sight-reduction formulas with distances and alignment. They are not extra inputs.

Example: for LAT 34°N, DEC 15°N, and LHA 40°W, the ordinary first intermediate is y ≈ 19° 16.7′. Co-latitude is 56°, so this same-name case gives Y ≈ 75° 16.7′. The guided sequence below now displays each Bygrave step.

Guided manual sequence

Bygrave sight reduction

Sight reduction turns your LAT, DEC, and LHA into the body's direction (true azimuth) and its height above the horizon (calculated altitude). The Bygrave gets there through several intermediate angles. Angle readouts use degrees and minutes to the nearest tenth: 34° 20.5′ means 34 degrees and 20.5 minutes of arc. There are 60 minutes in one degree. Numeric input fields use decimal degrees.

What each letter means

Lowercase y comes first. Uppercase Y comes next. For the example sight, you read y = 19° 16.7′ from DEC and LHA. Latitude 34° gives co-latitude 56°. Both LAT and DEC are north, so Y = 56° 00.0′ + 19° 16.7′ = 75° 16.7′.

Convert A → Zn · true azimuth

A is the angle read at L in step 3. Zn is the final bearing clockwise from true north. For this simulator's supported branch, use:

Your LATBody's directionConversion
NorthWestZn = 180° + A
NorthEastZn = 180° − A
SouthWestZn = 360° − A
SouthEastZn = A

Positive hour angle means west; negative means east. Example: LAT 34°N, H 40°W, A = 72° 12.7′ gives Zn = 180° + A = 252° 12.7′. Other branches need additional quadrant rules.
